Publications & Preprints

Scientific questions behind the work.

RiboCouple links RNA backbone generation to sequence design through a learned interaction topology Under review

Zhiqi Ma, Yefeng Zheng†

bioRxiv preprint, 2026

Scientific question: How can residue relationships inferred from sequence-free backbone geometry be preserved and transferred to sequence design?

Computational evidence: Pair-type decoding reached 45.1% joint refolding validity versus 33.5% for a matched topology-free decoder (550 generated backbones). Replacing backbone-aligned pair states with statistics-matched donors dropped validity from 46.4% to 21.4% under the same frozen decoder — decoding uses backbone-aligned pair information, not pair statistics alone.

The learned interface transfers to backbones from other generators without retraining, improves secondary-structure agreement on 94 native RNAs, and its pair confidence prioritizes candidates before external structure prediction.

siProGenA: Generative siRNA Candidate Construction via Position Proposal and Guide Generation Independently conceived

Zhiqi Ma*, Jiale Zhou*, Rubo Wang,Zhipeng Deng, Zhijian Wu, Yefeng Zheng†

bioRxiv preprint, 2026

Scientific question: Where to target · What guide sequence to design.

Biological hypothesis: Can controlled deviations from canonical complementarity improve the potency–specificity trade-off in siRNA design?

Computational evidence: Across 354 measured sites, constrained variants outscored canonical complements under all four evaluators (85.1% on average). Matched-random, GC, and secondary-structure controls support the comparison.

siProGenA reframes siRNA design as generative Where/What candidate construction, producing compact transcript-level libraries intended to reduce synthesis and screening burden.

Long-term research vision

Beyond benchmark optimization.

UnderstandLearning representations that capture biological structure, function, and constraints.
Generate & ReasonBuilding AI systems that generate molecular designs and reason about scientific hypotheses.
ValidateTesting computational predictions through independent evaluation and biological experiments.
DiscoverTurning validated models and hypotheses into new scientific insights and real-world applications.

My long-term goal is to develop reliable AI systems that move beyond black-box optimization toward scientifically grounded discovery. I am particularly interested in connecting generative models, scientific reasoning, biological validation, and research translation—and in building scientific agents that can support this iterative process. As AI takes on more of the search, I want the questions to stay human — these tools should extend a researcher's judgment, not replace it.

About

I am a researcher working at the intersection of generative AI, computational biology, and scientific discovery.

I received my M.Sc. in Bioinformatics from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and I am currently a Visiting Student at Westlake University.

My research focuses on generative models for RNA and therapeutic biomolecules, particularly sequence–structure co-design, siRNA generation, and reliable molecular optimization. Across these problems, I am interested in how structural and biological constraints can be incorporated into generative models, and how model-guided optimization can be evaluated reliably.
